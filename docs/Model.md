# Model Documentation

This document explains the machine learning pipeline that powers the Employee Attrition Prediction Platform.

## 1. Algorithm Selection
After testing multiple algorithms (Logistic Regression, Random Forest, SVM), **XGBoost (Extreme Gradient Boosting)** was selected due to its superior performance on non-linear, tabular data and its robust handling of missing values and class imbalance.

## 2. Handling Class Imbalance
Employee attrition datasets are inherently imbalanced (typically 80% stay / 20% leave). If untrained on imbalanced data, models become heavily biased toward the majority class, destroying recall.
- **Solution:** We implemented **SMOTE (Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique)** during the training phase to synthetically generate examples of the minority class, ensuring the model learns the distinct characteristics of employees who leave.

## 3. Feature Importance
Based on the trained XGBoost model, the top drivers of employee attrition in this dataset are:
1. **OverTime:** Working frequent overtime dramatically increases burnout and flight risk.
2. **Monthly Income:** Below-market compensation is a primary driver for seeking new opportunities.
3. **Total Working Years & Years At Company:** Newer employees have a higher turnover rate compared to tenured staff.
4. **Job Satisfaction:** Self-reported satisfaction metrics heavily correlate with retention.

## 4. Explainable AI (SHAP)
Because XGBoost is a complex ensemble model ("black box"), we use **SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations)** to interpret its decisions.
- Every prediction generated by the API passes through the SHAP `TreeExplainer`.
- The system extracts the top 5 features that pushed the probability higher or lower.
- This allows HR managers to see exactly *why* a specific employee was flagged as high risk (e.g., "This employee was flagged primarily because their Monthly Income is low and they are working OverTime").
